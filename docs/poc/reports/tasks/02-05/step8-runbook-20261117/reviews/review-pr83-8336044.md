## Analysis

### M-1: a start needs a UUID session

- **The check.** `session_uuid` accepts exactly the lowercase 8-4-4-4-12 form, and the unit test lists six bad cases. The guard is at `crates/ieo-coord/src/delivery.rs:791`. It runs only when `op == "start"`. That covers R1, G1, a spare with `new_session`, and a `recovery_allowed` start.
- **Where it sits.** The guard runs after the judgment and before the budget check (`admit_with`) and before `record_use` (`:942`). Nothing reaches the supervisor or the ledger, the spare is not consumed, and no recovery approval is used. `spare_e2e` step 0 asserts `not_called`, a reason containing "UUID", and an empty ledger.
- **Reconnect is unaffected.** The supervisor resumes with `--resume <checkpoint session>` (`crates/ieo-supervisor/src/args.rs:48-52`). The delivery's `session_id` is only the label `new_session_id`. Mutation V4 shows that no test pins this (I-B), but the RUNBOOK asks for a UUID everywhere anyway.
- **`not_called` after the delivery row is correct and documented.**
  - The row from `record_request` is kept, which is the same shape as every other `not_called`. REPORT §11.1 says "판단 뒤라 전달 행은 남는다".
  - The attempt id is now used: a retry needs a new `attempt_id` and a new key (`ATTEMPT_ID_CONFLICT`, `delivery.rs:463-477`). This is not written down, but RUNBOOK §7 already makes an unexpected `not_called` a stop-and-report (I-C).
  - The contract text (`contracts/ieo-api-v0.openapi.yaml:18-21`) and REPORT §11.1 "동작 변경" list the change.
- **RUNBOOK.** §5 (`RUNBOOK.md:76`) requires a new UUID per run, gives `uuidgen | tr A-Z a-z` or `/proc/sys/kernel/random/uuid` and a regex check, and explains the failure mode. §5 table rows `:84-86` and §6 item 5 (`:107`) repeat it.

### L-1: re-check before the call, and the wait re-reads its bound

- **The re-check.** `STOPPING` is checked again at `delivery.rs:964`, after the admission and right before `start_with`. It drops the admission without an in-flight marker and without a ledger record, so a spare is not consumed. The window between the re-check and the call is microseconds. U3, which removes the re-check, survives, but it is a race and fails closed.
- **The wait.** `wait_finishing_bounded` re-reads `LATEST` every 50 ms. `bound_reached` means: the fixed override when one is set, otherwise at least 780 s and not before the latest bound. The pure function is unit-tested with six cases (V3 is killed). There is no deadlock: `LATEST` is locked only for the copy.
- **The 900 s figure is right.**
  - A run accepted before the signal finishes within its bound, at most 780 s after the signal.
  - A delivery that passed the first check either answers within its 120 s in-flight window or meets the re-check as `not_called`.
  - So the 120 s goes on top of 780 s, and `-t 900` (`RUNBOOK.md:128`) has slack.
- **Residual.** The second wait in `main` (`bin/ieo-coord.rs:167`) can still add up to another 780 s outside podman. Under `-t 900` this is moot (I-F, carry-over).
- **New Low (L-A).** The re-check at `:964` comes after the recovery approval was used at `:942`. An approval is single-use: `role_recovery_uses.recovery_id` is the primary key, so a second use gets `RECOVERY_USED`. A delivery with a `recovery_id` that meets the re-check therefore spends the person's approval without a run. This fails closed and costs no budget. Step 8 plans no recovery, and the person can approve again. Moving the re-check before `record_use` fixes it.
